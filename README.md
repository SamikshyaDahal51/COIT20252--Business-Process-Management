## 1.	Introduction to Business process Management:

Business Process Management (BPM) is a disciplined, technology-enabled management method used to identify, create, execute, monitor, and govern both automated and manual operations. BPM helps companies achieve consistent, targeted results, generate value for customers, and more agilely link everyday operations with strategic business goals by purposefully enhancing and developing end-to-end processes.


## 2.	Fundamentals of Business Processes:

Business processes are structured groups of actions that convert inputs into useful results for clients or companies. They often cover several functional domains, start with a particular trigger, and include people, technology, or both. Clear goals, well-defined boundaries, measurable outcomes, and contributions to organisational effectiveness, quality, and customer happiness are all characteristics of well-designed business processes. Gaining an understanding of these principles provides the foundation for enhancing business performance through efficient business process management. (Anon., 2026)

## 3.	Business Process Management Concepts & Frameworks:

A systematic approach to the analysis, design, implementation, monitoring, and ongoing improvement of business processes is called business process management, or BPM. BPM frameworks offer organised techniques that support governance, standardisation, and performance measurement while assisting businesses in coordinating their operational procedures with strategic goals. BPM makes it possible for businesses to provide more value to customers and adjust to shifting market conditions through efficient management and ongoing improvement. (Anon., 2026)

## 4.	Purpose & Triggers of Process Analysis:

A key component in business process management is process analysis, which analyses how well current business procedures accomplish organisational goals. It finds chances for improvement as well as inefficiencies, bottlenecks, and pointless tasks. Before putting process improvements into place, organisations start process analysis for a number of reasons, such as declining performance, digital transformation, consumer expectations, technology breakthroughs, regulatory changes, and strategic business decisions. (APQC, n.d.)

## 5.	Analytical Dimensions & Techniques in Process Analysis

Business process performance is determined using both qualitative and quantitative methods using analytical dimensions and procedures. To find areas for improvement, analysts evaluate bottlenecks, handoffs, cycle times, expenses, risks, customer encounters, and process variances. Reliable evidence is provided by methods like process mining, root cause analysis, and performance measurement, which facilitate well-informed decision-making and ongoing business process improvement. (Anon., 2026)
